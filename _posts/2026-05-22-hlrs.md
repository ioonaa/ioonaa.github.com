What happens when science, technology, art, and society inspire one another?

<strong>theGATE</strong> is an initiative exploring exactly these intersections. From May 18–22, the <strong>High Performance Computing Center Stuttgart</strong> (HLRS) opened its doors for thought-provoking conversations and I was delighted to join the festival's eCulture Convention.

![Foto]({{ site.url }}/images/hlrs26.jpg "theGATE @HLRS"){:height="250" .center-image}

There's lots to...
From discussions about how physics can inspire art and how art can help us imagine what physics struggles to describe to reflections on how we visualise phenomena that are impossible to see and that challenge the way we understand reality itself.
From rethinking AI not only as \emph{Artificial Intelligence}, but also as \emph{African Intelligence}, to conversations about why digital sovereignty matters for the future of knowledge.
From digital monasteries and supercomputers in churches to the computational pain of cooling systems.

What stayed with me most was the atmosphere of curiosity: the sense that innovation does not only happen through faster machines or better algorithms, but also through dialogue, imagination, and the courage to ask unexpected questions.
